Three Novel Contributions:

1
Dual-Stream Disentanglement
Appearance decoder learns WHAT hazards look like. Context decoder (dilated conv) learns WHERE hazards occur. Never combined for single-RGB safety segmentation.
2
Hierarchical Conditional Inference
Stage 1 binary map is a direct structural input to Stage 2. Stage 2 knows WHERE hazards are before classifying WHAT they are.
3
Severity-Initialised Fusion Gates
Gate biases initialised from injury risk scores [0.0, 0.1, 0.6, 0.9]. High-severity classes rely more on spatial context.

Loss decreases consistently with no overfitting. Validation mIoU improves from 0.38 at epoch 1 to 0.50 at epoch 15, showing continued improvement trend.

Stage 1 Binary Map Analysis:

  • Stage 1 correctly activates over floor and obstacle regions
  • Low activation (near 0) on walls, ceilings and windows
  • Provides interpretable fast hazard alert output
  • Dual-stream features produce more accurate binary maps than single-decoder baselines

Sharp Objects Limitation:

Zero IoU due to extreme pixel sparsity (0.06% of test pixels, 46:1 imbalance). Class weighting (w=6.0) and COCO augmentation insufficient at this scale. Future work: patch-level oversampling.

8 Conclusion and Future Work

Summary:

DSPHN integrates DAHD dual-stream decoding with PHRN hierarchical inference into one architecture. Controlled ablation confirms DSPHN (mIoU 0.4198) outperforms DAHD-only (0.3894) and PHRN-only (0.3793), showing the integration is strictly better than either component alone.

Contributions:

  • Novel DSPHN architecture integrating dual-stream and hierarchical inference
  • First indoor hazard annotation dataset (100 images, Make Sense AI)
  • 4-class safety taxonomy by injury consequence
  • COCO sharp object augmentation strategy

Future Work:

  • Patch-level oversampling for Sharp Objects
  • Pretrained ResNet/EfficientNet backbone
  • Higher resolution inputs (512x512)
  • Real-time deployment on assistive robots
  • Temporal extension for video monitoring
